Lyrics, word by word.

Each word lights up as it is sung when the lyrics carry word times; otherwise the line being sung does. From an .lrc file beside the song, lyrics stored in the file, or the free LRCLIB database. Click a line to jump to it.

Made from the cover.

The progress bar, playing indicator and backdrop take their colour from the album art. Covers are kept sharp for large screens.

Hear exactly
what you own.

Every step from the file to your speakers is shown, and nothing changes the sound unless you ask it to.

Bit-perfect, when you want it.

Exclusive mode sends each song at its own sample rate, with no Windows mixing or resampling. The signal path says when the device gets exactly what is in the file.

Even volume, no clipping.

Loudness levelling measures every song once (EBU R128, or the file's ReplayGain) and keeps albums as they were mastered.

Tuned to your headphones.

A 10-band equalizer with presets, remembered for each output device. Plug your headphones back in and the music follows them.

Spotify playlist import

The playlist you love.
The files you own.

Pick one of your Spotify playlists, your Liked Songs or your top songs. Slate Music looks for the matching recordings in your local library, keeping Spotify's order.

Review uncertain matches, correct versions, and save the playlist. It can update itself from Spotify each time you open the app. Missing songs stay visible and out of the playable queue.

Read the connection guide ↗

Metadata matching only. No Spotify audio downloads. Connecting requires your own Spotify developer app and an eligible account.

The useful details.

Which audio formats can I play?+

FLAC, MP3, WAV, AAC/M4A, ALAC, Ogg Vorbis, Opus and AIFF have been tested. Raw AAC has limited seeking support. WMA, APE, DSD and protected files are not supported.

Music plays through Windows at 48 kHz stereo, or, with exclusive mode on, at each song's own sample rate with nothing changed on the way (bit-perfect). See audio details.

Does it really work offline?+

Yes. Playback, search, playlists, lyrics files and your saved library work offline. Songs without lyrics of their own are looked up online unless you switch that off in Settings. Other features that go online are optional: Spotify import, original years, artist photos, Last.fm and update checks. Each can be switched off in Settings.

What does Discord see?+

Nothing, until you switch on the Discord status in Settings. Then the Discord app on your PC shows "Listening to Slate Music" with the album cover, song and artist while music plays. The status goes only to that app and needs no Discord setup; to find the cover, the album and artist name are looked up in Apple's iTunes catalogue, Deezer's or MusicBrainz, which you can switch off.

Will it change my music files?+

No. Slate Music reads your files and stores its own library database separately. It does not retag, move, rename or delete your music. Playlists, favorites and virtual albums live in Slate’s database.

What do I need on Windows?+

Windows 10 or 11, a 64-bit x64 PC, WebView2 and a working audio output. WebView2 is usually already installed; setting it up on a new PC may need internet.

The installer does not currently have a Windows Authenticode publisher certificate, so Windows may show an unknown-publisher warning. Download only from the official GitHub release.

How do updates work?+

Updates are checked and downloaded automatically by default. Update files and their version are verified cryptographically. Installation requires confirmation while paused, or your choice to install on exit. Your library and settings are preserved.

Is Slate Music free?+

Yes. Slate Music is free and open source under the MIT license. No Slate account or subscription is needed. Spotify controls eligibility for its optional metadata API; see the Spotify setup guide.
